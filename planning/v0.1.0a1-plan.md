## 1. Project framing

`fresh-fuchs` (the FUCHS project) builds a stochastic, risk-aware forest
landscape planning prototype on the `femic-tsa29mini-instance` bundle: a
~100,000 ha square subset of TSA29 (Williams Lake), 90,499.8 ha total area,
35,083.0 ha managed land base after the proportional retention split, 21
analysis units (AU), 72 development types, 9,788 fragments, base year 2026,
30 periods of 10 years, max age 300.

The research question (from the notes) is a nested decision problem:

- **Outer problem (policy / administration).** Landscape-scale policy on
  (1) target species / development-type composition by area share and
  (2) harvest policy — AAC as a function of development type and rotation
  age. Evaluated risk-sensitively on the distribution of outcomes using
  downside-risk measures (CVaR).
- **Inner problem (enterprise / implementation).** Harvest scheduling and
  replanting decisions (which species are planted after harvest) that
  maximize NPV subject to the outer policy constraints. Formulated as a
  Model I linear program.

**Locked design decisions (2026-08-13):**

1. **Outer = full Monte-Carlo.** Sample disturbance (fire) realizations —
   and, later, wood-price realizations — and evaluate each outer policy
   configuration on the resulting distribution of NPV. Empirical
   distributions preferred over parametric Gaussian for downside-risk
   measures (per the notes' extreme-event paper findings).
2. **Inner = LP (adopted recommendation).** A Model I LP maximizing NPV,
   subject to even flow and the outer policy constraints, solved once per
   scenario (full foresight within a scenario). The oldest-first
   priority-queue heuristic from the demo notebook is retained as a
   validation baseline, not as the production inner solver.
3. **Full foresight within a scenario.** Fire events are fixed, scheduled
   events for a given scenario (encoded as ws3 actions/transitions), so one
   LP solve yields the schedule and NPV for that scenario. Recourse /
   rolling-horizon ("reactive vs proactive") planning is explicitly out of
   scope for v0.1.0a1 and tracked as a post-release extension.
4. **Species dimension enters the model (re-scoped P1.3).** The tsa29mini
   bundle carries no species-proportion curves (all 108 curves are
   `treated`/`untreated`; fragments have no species attribute), so age-varying
   proportions are unavailable for the mini case. Species enters as a static
   primary-species class per AU (from the AU table's `canfi_species` code:
   100=spruce, 204=lodgepole pine, 500=Douglas-fir), exposed as a composition
   surface (`instance/species.py`, `instance/composition.py`) that does not
   expand the ws3 model. Age-varying species curves remain a Phase 4 concern
   gated on upstream bundle data.
5. **Base case = tsa29mini.** The full TSA29 is the future scale-up target
   (cf. `fresh-salvage`), but the mini instance is the v0.1.0a1 case study.
6. **NPV surface is greenfield but anchored.** Revenue, harvest costs
   (fhops), replanting costs, and salvage economics must be built, anchored
   where possible to the fresh-salvage calibration (see section 5).

**Reuse, never re-implement** (the FRESH-ecosystem boundary contract):

| Package | What we consume | What we do NOT do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ws3` | `ForestModel`, `opt.Problem`/LP machinery, Model I even-flow, `apply_schedule`, actions/transitions, `advanced_modeling` reference types | re-implement a wood-supply engine |
| `femic` | tsa29mini bundle, `fmg/woodstock.py`, `ws3_bridge.py`, BTC log-grade banks, FANSIER parsing, price matrices | re-build instance pipelines |
| `fhops` | harvest cost estimation (`costing/machines.py`, `productivity/`, salvage systems, road costs) | re-estimate costs from raw FPDat |
| `nemora` | DBH distribution fit, `pdf_to_cdf`, `sample_distribution`, `bootstrap_inventory` | fit distributions from scratch |
| `freshforge` | workflow + matrix orchestration, evidence manifests | build a new orchestrator |
| `fresh-salvage` | economic calibration anchors, MFRI fire constants, agent-margin formulation | duplicate the subsidy model |

## 2. Architecture

```
instance/        femic tsa29mini bundle -> extended ws3 ForestModel
                 (species dimension, FIRE/SALVAGE/REPLANT actions/transitions)
                     |
                     v
economy/         NPV layer: revenue(volume x price, later log grades),
                 harvest cost (fhops), replanting cost, salvage economics,
                 discounting; typed price/cost surface
                     |
                     v
scenario/        full-MC generator: fire occurrence/extent/severity (MFRI),
                 optional price stochasticity, distribution registry
                 (Gaussian first; empirical bootstrap via nemora later)
                     |
                     v
inner/           Model I LP per scenario: max NPV s.t. even flow + outer
                 constraints + salvage feasibility + replant transitions;
                 oldest-first heuristic baseline
                     |
                     v
outer/           policy config (composition targets, AAC/rotation), grid
                 search driver, NPV-distribution metrics (expected, CVaR,
                 shortfall), policy ranking
                     |
                     v
orchestration/   freshforge workflow + matrix definitions, evidence
                 manifests, provenance records
```

Data flow in one sentence: for each outer policy configuration, generate N
MC scenarios; for each scenario solve the inner LP to a schedule and an NPV;
pool the N NPVs into a distribution; evaluate downside risk; rank policies.

Proposed package layout (mirrors the fresh-salvage module convention):

```
src/fresh_fuchs/
  instance/       bundle -> model bridge (femic), species-proportion wiring
  economy/        price/cost surface, cash-flow and NPV functions
  scenario/       fire dynamics, distributions, scenario generation
  inner/          lp (Model I build/solve/apply), heuristic baseline, schedule records
  outer/          policy config records, grid search, risk metrics (CVaR)
  orchestration/  freshforge provider + matrix helpers
  cli.py          Typer CLI, thin wrappers over the APIs
```

## 3. Definition of Done for v0.1.0a1

The v0.1.0a1 release ships an end-to-end, reproducible, validated prototype:

1. **End-to-end pipeline.** Given the tsa29mini bundle, the pipeline runs:
   extended model build -> MC fire-scenario generation -> inner LP solve per
   scenario -> NPV distribution -> policy grid search -> CVaR-based ranking.
   Every step runnable from the CLI (`fresh-fuchs` commands) and from the
   Python API.
2. **Deterministic regression anchors.** (a) The Phase-1 deterministic
   baseline reproduces the demo-notebook numbers within tolerance (managed
   land base 35,083.0 ha; 30-period mean annual harvest ~35,381 m3/yr for the
   volume-max even-flow LP); (b) one seed-fixed MC run produces bit-stable
   schedules/NPVs.
3. **Outer/inner coupling demonstrated.** At least one grid search over
   composition targets (and a rotation-age axis) ranks policies by
   expected-NPV and CVaR, with the recommended policy identified and the
   trade-off reported.
4. **Validation report.** `planning/validation-report.md` records anchors,
   tolerances, the remaining harvest-area discrepancy investigation, and all
   caveats (full-foresight optimism, coast-vs-interior price provenance).
5. **Economics calibration record.** `planning/economics-calibration.md`
   documents every price/cost/salvage constant and its provenance, including
   fresh-salvage cross-checks (salvage margin, subsidy flip turn-on).
6. **Governance, docs, CI, release.** README, AGENTS.md, ROADMAP.md,
   CHANGE_LOG.md, RELEASE_NOTES.md, Sphinx docs, CI (ruff/mypy/pytest/
   sphinx), release-artifact checks (build + twine), public-safe synthetic
   fixtures, tag `v0.1.0a1`.

## 5. Validation and calibration anchors

Deterministic anchors (from the demo notebook / ws3-vs-Patchworks):

- Managed land base: 35,083.0 ha (ws3) vs 35,083.2 ha (Patchworks) — exact
  after proportional retention split.
- 30-period mean annual harvest: ~35,381 m3/yr (ws3) vs ~34,094 m3/yr
  (Patchworks) — within ~4%.
- 3-period smoke profile is the fast deterministic regression gate; 30-period
  is the production validation horizon.

Economic anchors (from fresh-salvage calibration, TSA29 Interior):

- Salvage margin (unsubsidized): ~ -21 to -24 $/m3.
- Subsidy flip turn-on: ~23.85 $/m3 (ramp 23.9-24.1); FESBC 14-15 $/m3
  closes ~60% of the gap.
- Burned-price discount: 0.65; decay 0.85; MFRI zones as in section 4 P3.1.
- Green SPF prices 146/127/55; burned costs 56/38; stumpage surface.

These are anchors to *compare against*, not numbers to force-fit. Every
deviation is recorded with a reason.

## 8. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| 30-period LP solve cost times thousands of scenarios is too slow | Keep LP lean (Model I, HiGHS), parallel process pool, smoke vs production horizons; if needed, coarse scenarios for grid search and finer for final ranking |
| Species-proportion bridge inflates model size / complexity | Validate area/volume conservation in P1.3; gate model size growth |
| Interior price/cost data quality weak | Provenance on every constant; sensitivity sweeps; fresh-salvage anchors as sanity bounds |
| MC convergence of CVaR requires many scenarios | Record convergence curves in P5.2; empirical distribution option |
| ws3 disturbance-encoding limits (action/transition fidelity) | Fallback: FUCHS-owned scenario engine drives ws3 schedules (rolling-horizon style) — flagged as a phase-3 decision gate |
| Annex bundle unavailable in CI | Synthetic fixtures; bundle optional/integration-only tests |
